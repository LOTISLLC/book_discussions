2026 Reading Series: Your Democracy Survival Toolkit

Tools for Understanding, Endurance, & Action

This year's reading series is structured with intention.

This is a space to:

  • Understand what is happening
  • Recognize patterns across history and the present
  • Think together about meaningful action
  • Stay grounded without burning out

The Arc of the Year

This year follows a deliberate progression and asks questions: What are the systems and guardrails? How does belief intersect with policy and power? How does propaganda and authoritarianism function? What do we do now and what does effective civic action actually look like? How do we stay engaged without burning out?

Each book builds on the last — intellectually and emotionally.

How to Participate

You are welcome to join at any point in the year. If a particular book speaks to you, start there. And if reading a full book feels like too much, read what you can and come anyway. This group is about building literacy & strength together and we want your voice at the table.

Past Book Discussions

From Dictatorship to Democracy

Gene Sharp
📅 April 29, 2026 @ 7pm ET · 160 pages

A concise and practical guide to nonviolent resistance, exploring how power operates — and how it can shift when people act strategically. Originally written for activists under authoritarian regimes, this book offers a clear framework for understanding effective civic action and the role ordinary people play in shaping political outcomes.

Separation of Church and Hate

Andrew L. Seidel
📅 February 25 (pp. 1–148) · March 25, 2026 (pp. 149–283)

A deeply researched takedown of far-right Christian nationalism, examining how Christianity has been weaponized for political power and offering practical tools for engaging with fundamentalist arguments — using scripture, common sense, and sharp humor. A rallying cry for compassion and clarity.

The Constitution of the United States of America

📅 Two-part discussion, January–February 2026

A welcoming two-part discussion reading the Constitution together, then reflecting on what we notice, what we question, and how its words are being invoked in today's America. Come as you are — curious, passionate, uncertain, or simply ready to return to the foundation we're all standing on.

Black Skin, White Masks

Frantz Fanon
📅 December 30, 2025 (pp. 1–88) · January 28, 2026 (pp. 89–206)

An unsurpassed study of the Black psyche in a white world. A major influence on civil rights, anti-colonial, and Black consciousness movements around the world — hailed for its scientific analysis and poetic grace since its first publication in 1952.

Orientalism

Edward Said
📅 September 24, October 29, & November 19, 2025

A groundbreaking critique of the West's historical, cultural, and political perceptions of the East. Said traces the origins of "orientalism" to centuries of European dominance that defined "the orient" simply as "other than" the occident — entrenched views that continue to shape Western ideas today.

Democracy Awakening

Heather Cox Richardson
📅 July 30 & August 27, 2025

Acclaimed historian Heather Cox Richardson delves into the tumultuous journey of American democracy, tracing the roots of authoritarian tendencies to the earliest days of the republic. More than a history book — a call to action reminding us that democracy requires constant vigilance and participation from all of us.

Under the Skin

Linda Villarosa
📅 May 28 & June 25, 2025

From an award-winning writer at the New York Times Magazine and contributor to the 1619 Project, a landmark book revealing the toll racism takes on individual health and the health of our nation. Villarosa lays bare the forces in American health care and society that cause Black people to "live sicker and die quicker."

Mutual Aid: Building Solidarity During This Crisis (and the Next)

Dean Spade
📅 February 12, 2025

A grassroots theory of mutual aid — describing how it is a crucial part of powerful movements for social justice, and offering concrete tools for organizing: how to work in groups, foster collective decision-making, prevent and address conflict, and deal with burnout.

The Gun, the Ship, and the Pen

Linda Colley
📅 Four sessions: January 29, February 26, March 26, & April 30, 2025

A work of extraordinary range tracing the global history of written constitutions from the 1750s to the twentieth century. Colley uncovers the close connections between the making of constitutions and the making of war, highlighting neglected sites, unexpected players, and the ways constitutions crossed boundaries to spread across six continents.

On Tyranny: Twenty Lessons from the Twentieth Century

Timothy Snyder
📅 January 15, 2025

A call to arms and a guide to resistance, presented as twenty instructions on how to combat the rise of tyranny — "Defend institutions," "Remember professional ethics," "Believe in truth." Short (126 pages), urgent, and invaluable.

Eve: How the Female Body Drove 200 Million Years of Evolution

Cat Bohannon
📅 October 30, November 26, & December 28, 2024

With boundless curiosity and sharp wit, Bohannon covers the past 200 million years to explain the specific science behind the development of the female sex. A sweeping revision of human history — and an urgent corrective for a world that has focused primarily on the male body for far too long.

New York: The Novel

Edward Rutherfurd
📅 Four sessions: June 26, July 31, August 28, & September 25, 2024

A rich, engrossing saga celebrating America's greatest city, weaving together tales of families rich and poor, native-born and immigrant — from a tiny Indian fishing village through the Revolutionary War, the Gilded Age, the explosion of immigration, and the attack on the World Trade Center.

Caste: The Origins of Our Discontents

Isabel Wilkerson
📅 Four sessions: February 28 through May 29, 2024

Pulitzer Prize–winning author Isabel Wilkerson examines the unspoken caste system that has shaped America. Linking the caste systems of America, India, and Nazi Germany, Wilkerson explores eight pillars that underlie caste systems across civilizations and shows the ways that the insidious undertow of caste is experienced every day.

There There

Tommy Orange
📅 January 31, 2024

A wondrous and shattering novel following twelve characters from Native communities, all traveling to the Big Oakland Powwow, connected in ways they may not yet realize. A chorus of voices telling of the plight of the urban Native American — grappling with a complex and painful history, with an inheritance of beauty and spirituality.

The Dawn of Everything: A New History of Humanity

David Graeber & David Wengrow
📅 Monthly sessions, February 2023 – January 2024

A dramatically new understanding of human history, challenging fundamental assumptions about social evolution — from the development of agriculture and cities to the origins of the state, democracy, and inequality. Monumental in intellectual range, animated by curiosity, moral vision, and a faith in the power of direct action.

A People's History of the United States

Howard Zinn
📅 Monthly sessions, September 2021 – January 2023 (Chapters 9–25)

One chapter per month, on the last Wednesday at 7pm. A foundational text that tells American history from the perspective of ordinary people — workers, women, indigenous peoples, enslaved people, and immigrants — rather than rulers and elites.

The End of Policing

Alex S. Vitale
📅 Four sessions: April 1 – June 3, 2021

A rigorous examination of the role of police in American society and a challenge to the assumption that policing and prisons are the only answer to social problems. Vitale argues for a rethinking of the role police play in our communities.

Radical Dharma: Talking Race, Love, and Liberation

Rev. angel Kyodo williams, Lama Rod Owens & Jasmine Syedullah
📅 Five sessions: January 3 – February 28, 2021

A bold and necessary call for a new American Buddhism that is not separate from the racial suffering in our communities. The authors argue that the liberation promised by Buddhist practice must be fully integrated with the social liberation movements of our time.

The Color of Law

Richard Rothstein
📅 Four sessions: January 14 – March 18, 2021

A definitive account of how federal, state, and local governments imposed residential segregation on American cities through explicit policy — not mere private prejudice. A forgotten history with profound implications for understanding inequality today.
